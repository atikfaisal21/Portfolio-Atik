01 — About

Who Am I?

Researcher · Engineer · Innovator

I am a graduate of the Department of Materials and Metallurgical Engineering at BUET, one of Bangladesh's most prestigious engineering institutions. My research bridges the gap between theory and experiment — from DFT+SOC band structure calculations of 2D valleytronic materials to hands-on thin film deposition and nanoparticle synthesis.

My work spans an exciting range: predicting mechanical properties of high-entropy alloys using machine learning, modeling CVD-grown WS₂ monolayers, engineering SnO₂ thin films for photodiode applications, and developing catalysts for antibiotic removal from wastewater.

I am an active member of the Billah Multifunctional Nanomaterials Group and an associate researcher at AhmedLab (Buffalo State University). I hold a US Provisional Patent for a real-time image analytics platform for rolling mill stability management.

Currently seeking PhD opportunities in the USA to further pursue cutting-edge research in computational and experimental materials science.

07 — Projects

Current Projects

⚛️ Valleytronics — WTe₂/VSe₂ Heterostructure for Valley-Polarized Transport
🟢 Ongoing

Investigating 2H-TaSe₂ and WTe₂/VSe₂ heterostructures as valleytronic materials through DFT-based simulations incorporating spin-orbit coupling (SOC) and DFT+U corrections. A three-step workflow (geometry relaxation → SOC static run → non-SCF band structure) was optimized to study valley polarization induced via magnetic proximity effects. Results include Wannier tight-binding models, anomalous Hall conductivity, JDOS, NEGF transport calculations (Kwant), and FET device simulations. The study establishes TaSe₂ as a valley-active 2D layer, suitable for integration with ferromagnetic substrates.

Supervised by Dr. Saquib Ahmed, SUNY Buffalo State

🎞️ Thin Film Deposition of Doped SnO₂ for Photodiode Applications
📝 Manuscript Writing

Deposition of SnO₂ thin films on glass substrates with specific dopant concentrations to investigate conductive properties. A Schottky contact between pure SnO₂ and doped SnO₂ films is established to induce photoconductivity, addressing limitations of persistent photoconductivity. Experiments and characterizations complete. Status: Manuscript preparation in progress.
